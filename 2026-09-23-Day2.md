Mod 1: Communication in a connected world

1.1 Network Types

The internet isnt owned by any single group or individual, it is a world wide collection of connected networks, whom are all exchanging information between themselves using common standards.

Transmission can come in different ways: wired, wireless, satelites, etc.

Local Networks can come in various sizes, being a household with 1-2 devices, to a business using hundreds of devices at once.

Networks allow for rapid communication and access to information in an efficient and cheaper way compared to traditional forms of communication.

- Small Homes: a few computers connected to eachother and the interent
- SOHO (Small office and home office): Allows a computer in a home office to connect to a corporate network or access centralized/shared reasoruces.
- Medium to Large: Many computers connected to eachother and the internet
- World Wide: Millions of computers that are connected together on a global scale

Mobile Devices:
- Smartphones: can connect to the internet from almost anywhere, are able to be used as a camera, GPS, telephone, etc.
- Tablet: similar to smartphones, just with a wider screen surface area.
- Smartwatch: Smartphone for your wrist, can connect to a smartphone to provide the user with notifications. can also track things like, steps, heartrate, and other health statistics.
- Smart Glasses: Wearable computer in the form of glasses, able to display a HUD (head up display) for user interaction.

1.2 Data Transmission
oh brother they're teaching my what a bit is 

Volunteered Data- Data explicitly given by a subject
Observed Data- Data obtained from recording a subject's actions
Inferred Data- data gotten from a combo of volunteered and Observed data

Bits must be converted to signals that can be transported across a network.
Media: a physical medium on which the signals are transmitted. (Copper wires, fiber optic, etc)

A signal consists of eletrical and optical patterns that are transmitted from one device to another. Signals may be concerted multiple times before reaching its destination.

- Electrical Signals: transmission through eletrical pulses along a copper wire
- Optical Signals: Transmission converting eletrical signals to light pulses
- Wireless signals: Transmission using infared, microwave, or radio waves through the air.

1.3 Bandwidth and Throughput

Streaming and gaming require a fast and reliable stream of a high amount of bits. The amount of possible data a network can proccess at once is its bandwidth.

-KBPS: thousands
-MBPS: Millions
-GBPS: Billions

Throughput: the measure of the transfer of bits across the media over a period of time. Due to outside factors, throughput never usually reaches the same value as bandwidth.

Could be caused by: Types of data being transmitted, latency or travel time, amount of data being sent.

Throughput doesn't take into account the value or usefullness of the data being transmitted or recieved.

In a network, throughput cannot be faster than the slowest link from two devices trasmitting to eachother. AKA, it cannot be faster as the slowest connection in the network.
